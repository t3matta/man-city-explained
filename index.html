01

A transformation, then an investigation.

Start with the club, the competitions and the extraordinary rise that gives this case its significance.

From United’s shadow to a dynasty

Manchester City and Manchester United are different clubs in the same city. City had history and a devoted fanbase, but United was the internationally dominant neighbor. City had gone decades without a league championship.

In 2008, Abu Dhabi United Group, associated with Sheikh Mansour of Abu Dhabi’s ruling family, acquired City. Major investment followed. In 2012, City won the league for the first time in 44 years.[3]

Under Pep Guardiola, the team reached exceptional heights: the 2023 treble, then an unprecedented fourth successive English top-flight title in 2024.[4] The financial proceedings developed alongside those achievements.

Why unlimited wealth is not unlimited permission

An owner can afford to fund losses while the club still breaches a spending rule. Historical Premier League PSR generally allowed up to £105m of adjusted losses over three years for a club in the league throughout, subject to conditions. Qualifying academy and community expenditure received special treatment.[6]

Historical rules, historical conduct

UEFA also operated financial rules. The central case applies the rules relevant to the seasons charged. The Premier League replaced PSR with a new financial framework for 2026/27; that change does not itself decide historical compliance.[7]

03

What “115 charges” actually means.

A headline label for repeated obligations across multiple rules and seasons—not 115 unrelated conspiracies.

1

Financial reporting

Were revenue, expenses and the club’s financial position accurately disclosed?

2

Player and manager pay

Were the complete compensation arrangements included in the required disclosures?

3

UEFA financial compliance

Did City comply with the European financial rules it was obliged to observe?

4

Premier League spending compliance

Did the club remain within the domestic profitability and sustainability requirements?

5

Cooperation with the investigation

Did City provide the documents and assistance required by the league?[8]

The scope
2009 → 2018

The main financial period ran from 2009/10 through 2017/18. Cooperation allegations extended into the subsequent investigation.[8]

A sports disciplinary case

This is not a criminal trial. The Premier League brought the proceedings, and an independent commission determined the allegations.

From allegation to finding

Today’s position differs from years of earlier reporting: an adverse ruling now exists. Its durability on appeal and the consequences remain unresolved.[1]

How did the allegations come to light?

In 2018, German publication Der Spiegel published internal communications obtained through Football Leaks, raising questions about the true sources of City’s income. City objected to hacked material and argued that communications lacked context. Investigations followed.[5]

The leak was the starting point of public scrutiny. The later commission’s decision is the relevant adjudicated finding, subject to City’s intended appeal.

04

Three proceedings. Different questions.

“But didn’t City already win?” refers to a different case. This is the source of much of the confusion.

2020 • appeal won

The UEFA case

European rules → Court of Arbitration for Sport

UEFA imposed a two-year European competition ban and €30m fine. CAS overturned the ban: principal allegations were unproved or too old under UEFA’s time limits.

A real win, with an important qualificationA non-cooperation finding remained, with a reduced €10m fine.[9]
2026 • appeal intended

The central case

Premier League → independent commission

Charges were brought in February 2023. The 2024 hearing led to the adverse findings published on September 29, 2026.

The current scandalFinancial breaches found; City contests the result; sanctions remain open.[1][2]
2025 • settled

The sponsorship-rule dispute

Associated Party Transaction rules

City separately challenged rules for transactions involving parties linked to a club’s ownership. The dispute ended in a September 2025 settlement.

A different legal questionCity accepted the then-current rules as valid and binding. That settlement did not resolve historical accounting compliance.[10]
Why the 2020 appeal does not settle the current case

“Completely cleared of everything” overlooks the cooperation finding and allegations excluded by the time limit. “Escaped entirely on a technicality” overlooks allegations that were not established on the evidence.[9]

The Premier League proceeding has a different framework and did not have UEFA’s equivalent five-year time bar.[11] An appeal victory in one proceeding does not automatically resolve another.

Commission’s position

The published findings stand for now.

The ruling found serious financial breaches. It concluded that accurate reporting would have revealed substantial breaches of both domestic and European spending limits.[1]

That is a disciplinary finding, subject to appeal—not merely an accusation in a newspaper.

City’s position

The decision is wrong.

City says it is innocent and has compelling evidence. It intends to challenge the decision for material errors of law, principle and fact, while also questioning the regulator’s impartiality.[2]

Those are the club’s assertions. They have not yet overturned the findings.

A financial advantage can compound.

This is the sporting logic behind the outrage. It is not a calculation of how many trophies the disputed funding caused.

Success can create legitimate future income.

That is why an early financial advantage can matter long after the original investment. The effect can persist through recruitment, squad depth, qualifications and reputation.

Exceptional players and coaching do not answer whether the financial rules were obeyed. Both sporting excellence and regulatory breaches can be relevant to the same period.

The limit of the inference

You cannot rerun football history.

Removing the disputed funding from a spreadsheet does not tell us who would have won every match or title. The financial findings do not establish an alternative league table.

Punishing a breach, assessing compensation and reallocating trophies are separate questions.

06

The verdict is not the punishment.

As of this edition, City has not been fined, deducted points, expelled or stripped of titles as a sanction in this case.

Possible consequences • not predictions
FineA financial penalty. The amount, if any, has not been determined.
Points deductionReduces league points; a large deduction could contribute to relegation.
ExpulsionAn extreme possible outcome discussed in reporting. It has not been ordered.
Past titlesNo automatic transfer of trophies to rivals follows from today’s findings.

The commission has broad sanctioning powers. The available possibilities do not tell us which will be selected.[1][12]

City’s deadline to appeal

The club has announced its intention to challenge the findings. A separate sanctions hearing is required.[1][2]

No fixed final-resolution date is established here.

The case is about whether a football dynasty’s finances were honestly reported and compliant. A commission has now ruled against City. City is fighting that ruling. The sporting consequences remain undecided.
